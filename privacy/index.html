Privacy

Last updated 17 October 2026

Tikkle is a budget for two people who run one home together. This notice explains, in plain words, what happens to what you put in.

The short version

  • We (the people who make Tikkle) do not collect your data. There are no Tikkle accounts, no Tikkle servers holding your budget, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • Your budget is kept on your iPhone and in Apple's iCloud, under your own Apple ID. We cannot open it.
  • What you share with your partner, you share through iCloud, directly between your two Apple IDs.

What Tikkle keeps, and where

On your iPhone and in your own iCloud

  • What you add to your budget: spends (amount, note, date, category, card nickname, tags, who it was for, and whether it was paid back), money in (amount, source, note), refunds, bills and subscriptions, monthly plans (what you plan to spend and the money you expect in) and month closes, assets and their values, goals, your categories and groups with their names and colours, and the categories a place usually goes in.
  • Your first name as you type it, and its initial.
  • A receipt photo, if you add a spend by taking one. It is made smaller and kept with that spend.
  • A number Apple gives Tikkle for your iCloud account. It is not your email or phone number, it means nothing outside Tikkle, and it only tells Tikkle which person in the household is you.

Shared with your partner

  • When you invite your partner, Tikkle uses Apple's own iCloud sharing. The household's Us items (Us spends and their receipt photos, Us bills, plans and goals), the categories and groups, the list of cards you add in Settings (names like "Amex"), and every asset except the ones you set to Personal are shared between your two Apple IDs. Your partner sees them and can change them, and so can you.
  • Your partner sees the card list when choosing how a spend was paid. A card you use only on Personal spends stays on your iPhone.
  • The money each of you expects in a month (for example a paycheck) is part of the household's plan, so you both see it and can change it.
  • When your partner accepts, iCloud tells Tikkle their first name so it can fill in their name for them. They can change it.
  • Personal items stay with you. A Personal spend, bill, plan, goal or month close is kept only in your own iCloud and never goes to your partner's iPhone.
  • Assets are shared with the household even when they belong to one of you; each shows whose it is. An asset you set to Personal (under Belongs to, or on Keep Personal before you invite your partner) is the exception: it moves to your own iCloud with its values and their notes, and it is not on your partner's Wealth, net worth, goals or export.

Only on this iPhone, never in iCloud

  • Your choices in Settings: Light, Dark or Auto, Lock with Face ID, the other currencies you use, and the goal you picked for Home.
  • Exchange rates Tikkle has already fetched.
  • The number Apple gave Tikkle for your iCloud account, so Tikkle can open at once next time.

Export

If you choose Export in Settings, Tikkle makes a spreadsheet file (CSV) on your iPhone and hands it to the iPhone's share sheet. It holds only what you can see in Tikkle: the household's items and your own Personal ones. Never your partner's Personal items. It goes only where you send it (for example Files, Mail or AirDrop). Tikkle keeps no copy anywhere else and sends nothing to us.

What goes over the internet, and to whom

  • Apple iCloud, to keep your budget and to share the household with your partner. Apple's own privacy policy covers iCloud.
  • Exchange rates. If you use more than one currency (your budget's and, say, won or yen), Tikkle asks Frankfurter (api.frankfurter.dev), a free service that passes on the European Central Bank's daily reference rates, for those currencies' rates. The request contains only the currency codes and the dates it needs. No amount, no place and nothing about you is sent. Like any website, Frankfurter can see your iPhone's internet address when it answers.
  • Privacy and Help pages. If you open Privacy or Help from Settings, your browser opens a page on GitHub Pages (tikkle-budgeting.github.io). Like any website, it can see your iPhone's internet address. Nothing from your budget is sent.
  • Nothing else. Tikkle has no other server and uses no other company's code.

Camera, receipts and Face ID

  • Camera. Tikkle asks for the camera only when you tap the camera button to add a receipt photo to a spend. The photo is made smaller and kept with that spend. Tikkle does not read the text on it.
  • Face ID. If you turn on Lock with Face ID, your iPhone checks your face or passcode itself. Tikkle only keeps the on or off switch and never receives anything about your face.

Notifications

Tikkle does not send you notifications. Your iPhone may get silent signals from iCloud that tell Tikkle your partner changed something, so the budget stays up to date.

Deleting your data

  • Inside Tikkle you can delete any spend (with its refunds), bill, goal (with its saved amounts) or asset (with its past values), one past value of an asset, a card from your list, and a group (its categories move to another group).
  • Deleting the app removes what is kept on this iPhone.
  • What is kept in your iCloud can be deleted in your iPhone's Settings: open your iCloud storage, choose Tikkle and delete its data.
  • Items in a shared household are kept in the iCloud of the person who started it. Deleting an Us item or a shared asset in Tikkle removes it for both of you.

Children

Tikkle is made for adults who run a household. It is not meant for children.

Changes and contact

If what Tikkle does with data changes, this notice will change first, with the date at the top. Questions: tikkle.app@icloud.com
